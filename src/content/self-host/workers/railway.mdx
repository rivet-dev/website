Workers run in **runner** mode here: your service opens a connection out to the control plane on startup. It needs no public domain and nothing pasted into a connect form.

## Requirements

- A [Railway account](https://railway.app)
- A GitHub repository containing your app
- A control plane, either [Rivet Cloud](https://dashboard.rivet.dev) or <SelfHostLink to="control-plane">your own</SelfHostLink>

## Steps

<Steps>

<Step title="Deploy the repository">

Connect your GitHub account to Railway and select the repository. Railway detects and deploys it. See [Railway's quick start](https://docs.railway.com/quick-start).

<ProductSlot name="snippet" />

</Step>

<Step title="Set the environment variables">

Add `RIVET_ENDPOINT` and `RIVET_PUBLIC_ENDPOINT` as service variables. Both values are described in <SelfHostLink to="workers">Workers</SelfHostLink>. See [Railway's variables docs](https://docs.railway.com/guides/variables#service-variables).

If your control plane is another Railway service, reference its private domain rather than a public one.

</Step>

<Step title="Set the draining period">

Railway kills the old deploy 0 seconds after `SIGTERM` by default, so actors are cut off mid-drain on every deploy.

Under **Settings → Deploy**, set **Draining seconds**. Actors need up to 30 minutes, so `2100` is the safe value. See [Railway's deployment teardown docs](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/deployment-teardown).

</Step>

<Step title="Verify">

Open the dashboard and confirm your service appears under **Runners**. It reconnects automatically if the connection drops.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Signal handling

If your start command is a wrapper (`npm start`, `yarn start`, a shell script), the wrapper becomes PID 1 and swallows `SIGTERM`, so the drain never runs regardless of the draining period.

- Invoke the binary directly, for example `node dist/index.js`.
- Or run `dumb-init` or `tini` as PID 1 in your Dockerfile.

## Scaling

Runner-mode workers are interchangeable, so raising the service's replica count adds capacity with nothing to re-register. New replicas dial out and appear under **Runners**.

Removing a replica is the slow direction: it drains for up to 30 minutes, so keep the draining period at `2100` and reduce replica count gradually rather than in one step.

Railway has no CPU-target autoscaler, so replica count is fixed until you change it. Size it for peak traffic, or use <SelfHostLink to="workers/kubernetes">Kubernetes</SelfHostLink> or <SelfHostLink to="workers/aws-ecs">AWS ECS</SelfHostLink> where the count moves with load.

## Settings to avoid

<Warning>
Do not enable [App Sleeping](https://docs.railway.com/reference/app-sleeping) on a worker. Sleeping suspends the process, which drops its connection to the control plane, and Railway only wakes a service on inbound HTTP. Worker traffic is outbound, so the service would never wake and actors could not be scheduled to it.
</Warning>

[Multi-region deploys](https://docs.railway.com/reference/deployment-regions) are supported and place workers closer to your users.
