---
title: "Custom Platform"
description: "Run Rivet nodes on any platform that can run your app."
---

A node is your app plus two environment variables. Any platform that can run your app and reach the control plane can host it.

## Requirements

A namespace and a node pool created with `PUT /pools/{pool}` (see [Nodes & Node Pools](/docs/nodes#quickstart)). In local development, RivetKit creates the `default` pool for you.

Your platform must provide:

- **A long-running process** for your app, as a container or a plain process.
- **Environment variables**, to carry the connection.
- **Outbound network access** to the control plane.

## Steps

<Steps>

<Step title="Deploy your app">

Deploy however your platform expects. A node listens on no port, so it needs no public URL.

</Step>

<Step title="Set the environment variables">

```bash
RIVET_ENDPOINT=https://<namespace>:<rivet-token>@<control-plane-host>
```

On a self-hosted control plane, the Rivet token is the value of its `auth.admin_token` setting. See <SelfHostLink to="nodes">Deploy Nodes</SelfHostLink>.

</Step>

<Step title="Configure graceful shutdown">

Each Rivet Actor gets up to 15 seconds to save its state. Give the process at least 60 seconds between `SIGTERM` and `SIGKILL`, and make sure the signal reaches it: a wrapper such as `npm start` becomes PID 1 and swallows it.

</Step>

<Step title="Verify">

Call one of your Rivet Actors from a client.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Troubleshooting

**Rivet Actors stay pending.** Check outbound network access, and that `RIVET_ENDPOINT` carries the right namespace and token. Check the node's logs for connection errors.

**The connection drops every 30 to 60 seconds.** A proxy in front of the control plane is timing out idle WebSockets. Raise its idle timeout to `3600` seconds. See <SelfHostLink to="control-plane/ports">Ports</SelfHostLink>.
